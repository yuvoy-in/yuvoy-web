There is a word for the feeling: **sonder**. The awareness that every person you pass is living a life as full and vivid as your own — the boatman checking his engine, the cook already three dishes into the morning, the child running late for a class you'll never see.

Tourism is built to keep that feeling out. It arranges a place into a set of views, and moves you between them on a schedule. You leave with photographs of a surface.

We are trying to build the opposite.

## To Yuvoy

To Yuvoy is to enter a place not as a tourist, but as a temporary participant in its actual life. To join the day rather than observe it. To be, briefly, one of the lives that make the place what it is.

> You belong to a place, briefly, rather than pass through it.

That is a small promise and a hard one. It means fewer people, slower days, and hosts who are not performing their home but simply living in it, with you alongside.

## What it asks of us

It asks us to sell less and mean more. To resist the brochure version of the Andamans — and to tell only the stories we've genuinely earned, from a place we actually operate in.

If we get it right, you won't come back with a list of things you saw. You'll come back with a few hours you lived.
